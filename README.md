## AI + Sports

The models are weakest exactly where the stakes are highest. Two tracks, sixteen 45-minute sessions:

- **Director track (6)** - what AI changes across performance, medical, recruitment and commercial; the data estate and who owns each kind of it; recruitment and valuation; athlete welfare and data rights; fans, commercial and the integrity rails; building a department coaches listen to.
- **Analyst track (10)** - one club: Rivermouth FC, three analysts, six seasons of event data, one season of tracking, and a head coach who has never opened a dashboard. Ten sessions build one match report that coach will actually read.

A live shot-quality model runs on the key pages. Toggle features - distance, angle, body part, defender pressure, keeper position - and watch the held-out accuracy climb from 50% to 83% with the gap to the fitted number holding steady at two points. The sixth toggle is shooter reputation: the fitted number jumps to 96% and the held-out number falls to 77%, opening the gap to nineteen. Report one number and you would have shipped it.

Taught from the public sports-analytics evidence base: shot-quality modelling and tracking-data research, and the systematic reviews of machine-learning injury-risk prediction, which range from AUC 0.52 to 0.87 and conclude that outputs are supportive information only, never used in isolation for selection or return-to-play decisions. Medical, welfare and data-protection content is orientation, not medical or legal advice. Betting strategy is out of scope.
